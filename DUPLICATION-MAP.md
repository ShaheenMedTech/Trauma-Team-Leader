# Structural Editing Rules

## Rule 1 — Preserve substance

No clinically meaningful content is deleted simply because it is duplicated.

## Rule 2 — Establish canonical locations

Each major concept receives one authoritative explanation.

## Rule 3 — Convert repetition into application

Repeated material becomes scenario-specific application.

## Rule 4 — Preserve clinical depth

Clinical chapters retain appropriate injury-specific detail.

## Rule 5 — Separate clinical knowledge from leadership knowledge

Clinical chapters explain:

- recognition
- assessment
- diagnosis
- immediate treatment
- definitive management

Leadership chapters explain:

- prioritisation
- coordination
- delegation
- communication
- decision-making
- anticipation
- reassessment
- escalation
- team performance

## Rule 6 — Avoid circular explanation

A later chapter should not repeatedly redefine a concept already established
earlier.

## Rule 7 — Use cross-references strategically

When a later chapter requires a previously established framework, reference it
briefly rather than reproduce it.

## Rule 8 — Renumber only after consolidation

Final chapter numbering is performed after duplicate-content decisions are
complete.

# Final Quality Test

Before final renumbering, every major concept should answer:

1. Where is it first introduced?
2. Where is it explained in full?
3. Where is it applied?
4. Where is it unnecessarily repeated?
5. Can the repeated material be converted into application?
6. Does the reader gain something new from every repetition?

The final manuscript should feel cumulative rather than repetitive.
